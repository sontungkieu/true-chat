\section{Offline Selector / Bandit-Style Baselines}

The selector baselines choose among logged candidates only. They do not replace the runtime adaptive heuristic. The main policies are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{fixed}: choose one frequent signature; low coverage.
  \item \code{cheapest}: choose the lowest resource-cost action.
  \item \code{best\_average}: choose the exact action signature with highest train mean reward.
  \item \code{family\_smoothed\_best\_average}: back off from exact signature to retrieval-context family to context policy.
  \item \code{shrinkage\_smoothed\_best\_average}: shrink exact/family/context means toward broader means.
  \item \code{linear\_reward\_model}: ridge/linear offline selector over non-leaking action and cost features.
  \item \code{smoothed\_linear\_selector}: add train-only aggregate means/counts to the linear selector.
  \item \code{oracle\_logged}: logged-candidate upper bound, not deployable.
\end{itemize}

The six-seed answer-only sweep showed that \code{best\_average} has the highest non-oracle reward when it covers a group, but coverage is 0.911. \code{shrinkage\_smoothed\_best\_average} is the strongest full-coverage non-oracle selector so far: reward 0.602, quality 0.773, oracle gap 0.070. The learned linear selectors are functional but not yet robustly stronger than simple smoothed baselines.
